<div class="section-title">./Themes</div>

<div class="row"><div class="key">01 / exposures</div><div class="val body"><strong>Integrative multi-omics of environmental exposures.</strong><br>Environmental exposures contribute substantially to interindividual variation in health and disease.<br>We use integrative multi-omics approaches to characterize how diverse exposures are biologically embedded across molecular layers, with particular attention to sex-specific effects.<br>Our work aims to generate mechanistic hypotheses linking exposure profiles to downstream biological pathways, including projects on tobacco smoke, occupational exposures, and other environmental stressors.</div></div>

<div class="row"><div class="key">02 / sex differences</div><div class="val body"><strong>Multi-omics characterization of sex differences in neurodegenerative disease.</strong><br>Sex differences are a major source of heterogeneity in neurodegenerative disorders, including Alzheimer's disease, yet their molecular bases remain poorly resolved.<br>We apply integrative multi-omics analyses to identify sex-specific molecular signatures associated with disease risk, progression, and phenotypic variation.<br>This work emphasizes mechanistic insight across molecular, clinical, and cognitive levels.</div></div>

<div class="row"><div class="key">03 / society</div><div class="val body"><strong>Cross-cutting: Ethical and societal dimensions of integrative omics research.</strong><br>The rapid expansion of multi-omics and integrative biomedical research raises important ethical, social, and epistemological questions.<br>We engage with these issues by examining how biological knowledge production intersects with environmental injustice, climate-related change, and social inequities.<br>This work draws on interdisciplinary bioethics perspectives to inform responsible and context-aware biomedical research.</div></div>
